Regime Conditioning in Crude Calendar Spreads

A walk-forward study of regime-conditional models across six crude spreads, the five ablations built to rescue the hypothesis, and the unconditional baseline that beat all of them.

Rohith Pranav Peruka IIT Mandi Built at Futures First Live system Source

Summary. PULSE is an end-to-end systematic research stack for crude calendar spreads: 35 financial and alternative data feeds, a point-in-time feature matrix, a seven-model competition run inside each regime cell, an expanding-window walk-forward over 2018 to 2026, and a live paper book reconciled by scheduler during market hours.

The central hypothesis was that crude spreads mean-revert differently across market regimes, so a regime-conditioned model should beat one that ignores regime. It did not. An unconditional rolling z-score returned a NET Sharpe of +0.372 against +0.293 for the pooled regime book. Five further ablations failed to close the gap. The deployed configuration applies regime conditioning only to the two instruments where it measurably earned its place, reaching parity at +0.374.

This note reports that negative result as the headline, alongside the two alternative data signals that did survive grading and the defects found when the system was audited against its own output.

01 Research question

Crude calendar spreads are a carry instrument. The front spread prices the market's willingness to hold a barrel for a month, so its behaviour should depend on physical state: whether the curve is in contango or backwardation, whether inventories are tight or in surplus, and whether realised volatility is stressed.

That motivates a testable claim:

Hypothesis Mean reversion in crude spreads is regime-dependent, so a model conditioned on curve shape, inventory level and volatility will outperform an unconditional one.

The universe is six instruments: Brent and WTI, each at M1–M2, M3–M6, and the 1–2–3 butterfly. The regime grid is three axes at three levels each, giving 27 composite cells, with a coarser three-cell pooled grid used for the live book.

02 Data

Roughly 35 sources, normalised into a DuckDB parquet lake with per-source health and provenance tracking. Three of them are non-price and were built specifically to test whether alternative data carries tradeable information here.

GDELT news corpus 3,564 headlines Geopolitical event study (see §05)

The geopolitical layer is not a sentiment score. It is a hand-built registry of 37 geolocated physical assets, each carrying a signed prior over nine price nodes, with an LLM extractor resolving every headline into a validated {asset, event_type, severity} triple before it reaches the study.

03 Method

Validation protocol first, because it constrains every number that follows.

  • Expanding-window walk-forward. 34 quarterly refits, 2018 to 2026. No model ever sees data past its own cutoff.
  • Point-in-time features. Every feature is built from data available at the timestamp it is attached to. Tests assert prefix stability, so a value computed at day t cannot change when future data is appended.
  • Causal regime labelling. The HMM forward filter at day d sees only data up to d. Gate decisions at each cutoff use only trades that had already closed.
  • Costs before conclusions. A single cost module defines per-spread round-trip cost, imported by both the backtest and the live A/B harness so the assumption cannot drift between them.
  • Mirrored decision rule. The gate is defined once and imported by both the live engine and the backtest. An invariant test asserts they stay bit-for-bit identical, so tuning the live gate without re-running the walk-forward fails the suite.

Estimators

Seven models compete inside every regime cell and the data selects the winner: Ridge, Lasso, ElasticNet, Huber, XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost, each producing a point estimate plus p10/p90 quantile bands. Regime detection was additionally tested with a Gaussian mixture plus causal sticky-HMM forward filter over curve level and its 5-day change. Conditional volatility was modelled with GARCH(1,1) and GJR-GARCH under Student-t innovations, refit every 21 days and scored on QLIKE.

04 Results

All figures are NET of round-trip transaction cost and annualised across the full 2018 to 2026 walk-forward.

Primary result A rolling z-score with no regime awareness returned a higher NET Sharpe than every regime-conditional variant tested.

Ablations

Rather than report the first result and stop, each plausible rescue of the hypothesis was built and measured.

AblationNET SharpeReading
Unconditional baseline +0.372 Reference
Collapse grid, regime as one-hot feature +0.380 Tied. The split, not the information, was the constraint
Logistic transitions instead of hard cuts +0.297 Tied with pooled. Threshold discontinuity was not binding
GMM plus sticky-HMM learned regimes +0.289 Worse. Learning the boundary does not beat the trader's cut
Lasso stability-selection feature pruning +0.308 Worse on Sharpe, but localises where signal lives
Portfolio vol targeting +0.198 Max drawdown cut 60%. Drawdown management, not alpha
Per-spread gate (deployed) +0.374 Parity. Regime enabled on 2 of 6 instruments only

The deployed configuration abandons the idea that regime conditioning should be applied uniformly. Instead each instrument is asked, at each refit cutoff and using only trades closed before it, whether its regime leg beat its baseline leg out of sample. Regime survives on two of six: WTI M1–M2 and the WTI butterfly. The result is stable across a margin sweep of 0 to 0.25 and a sample floor of 10 to 30, which is the evidence that it is a selection rule rather than a fitted parameter.

05 Signals that survived grading

Every empirical layer uses the same gate: serve a measured coefficient only when it clears |t| ≥ 2 on a minimum sample and passes a directional sanity check. Otherwise the interface shows a labelled economic prior. Two findings cleared it.

Inventory surprises are regime-dependent, and today's regime is the wrong one

Conditioning regimeDirectional accuracySignificance
Crude, high stocks (glut)75–81%p < 0.01
Crude, backwardated (current)52%n.s.
Gasoline, backwardated (current)57%p = 0.008
Gasoline, large surprises63–68%p < 0.001

The operational consequence is an abstention rule. In the current regime the framework declines to make a directional crude call and redirects conviction to gasoline, where the edge is measured. A model that produces a crude call every Wednesday regardless of regime is producing noise three quarters of the time.

Supply shocks price into distillate, not flat price

Across 112 graded geopolitical events and 1,414 node-level claims, chokepoint disruption firms the ULSD crack 57% of the time over five days (n = 229, p = 0.047). Crude flat price spikes on day one and reverts by day five: the risk premium round-trips while physical tightness persists in the refined product.

Data quality dominated model choice A keyword extractor could not separate “Hormuz closes” from “Hormuz reopens.” Same asset, same disruption vocabulary, opposite sign. Replacing it with structured LLM extraction multiplied the gradeable sample by 2.3× and turned a null result into a measured edge.

Benchmark selection was tested, not assumed

US crude inventories move WTI roughly 17 times more than Brent (β +0.026 versus +0.0015). The framework headlines WTI on inventory releases because the regression says WTI, not because crude is conventionally quoted in Brent.

06 Research integrity audit

A system that computes its own P&L has every incentive to flatter itself. The stack was audited against its own output; four defects materially changed reported performance.

Reported P&L was inflated by a factor of roughly 25,000

The paper book headline read +$110,179. Of those rows, 374 were walk-forward replay trades sized at 4,000 to 22,000 barrels, sharing a table with the live book and drowning it. The genuine live book was 10 trades and −$4.26.

Fixed: performance defaults to the live book; replay sits behind an explicit flag.

Win rate and its own breakdown disagreed

The headline read 43.8% beside a breakdown of 166W / 69L, which implies 70.6%. The denominator counted 144 break-even scratches that the numerator excluded.

Fixed: win rate measured over decisive trades; scratches reported separately.

A significant factor was significant for the wrong reason

The geopolitical news factor cleared every threshold with t = 3.0, but had an 18% directional hit rate. The t-statistic came from a small cluster of outlier war days, not from a persistent relationship.

Fixed: the measured gate now also requires hit rate above 50% and a lexicon-consistent sign.

A concurrency fault silently disabled the engine

A shared DuckDB connection executed concurrently by warm-up and scheduler threads corrupted a cached frame at boot. The regime engine then failed on every request until the process restarted, zeroing the live A/B tick without raising an alert.

Fixed: thread-local cursors, lock-guarded cache, schema validation before any frame is cached.

A cost-aware entry gate was added in the same pass. The live ranker now refuses any entry whose expected take-profit does not clear twice the round-trip cost. The rule was motivated by a real signal: a top-ranked WTI M1–M2 entry with a $0.03 take-profit against $0.03 of round-trip cost, a trade that nets zero when it wins.

07 Production

The research does not stop at a backtest. The same decision logic that the walk-forward validates is the logic that runs live.

The system has run publicly and unattended on a containerised deployment, accumulating a live forward book as out-of-sample confirmation of the walk-forward verdict.

08 Limitations

Stated here rather than discovered by the reader.

  • The tuned exit rule is in-sample. Its 82.9% win rate falls to roughly 74–75% out of sample. Sharpe and profit factor from that rule should be read as optimistic.
  • The geopolitical study is a single episode. 112 events from one 2026 Hormuz escalation, with clustered and overlapping forward windows. The p-values are optimistic; the direction and cross-node coherence are the signal.
  • Pre-2021 WTI settlements are synthesised from one-minute mids rather than real daily settles. Everything derived from them is flagged as an estimate in the interface, and the WTI glut regime is consequently untested.
  • The live forward book is slow. With a 30-trading-day time stop it cannot reach significance quickly, so it is confirmation of a question the walk-forward already answered on 13,758 closed trades, not the primary evidence.
  • The conflict-intensity axis is degenerate on this corpus. ACLED coverage ends before the episode being studied, so every event maps to one stale level. The machinery is validated on synthetic data; the real test needs more data, not more code.
